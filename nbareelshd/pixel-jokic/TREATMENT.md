# Pixel Jokić: "Nobody wanted him" (16-bit Pixel RPG, vertical)

180×320 native pixels ×6 = 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~20 s, Spanish, the user's cloned narrator voice.

## Research applied
- **yt-viral** (162 shorts, 8 Spanish NBA channels): the outliers (2.5–6.8× their channel median) are a **claim or question about a current star with conflict** ("…is the NBA's problem", "the TRUTH about OKC", "they broke the unwritten rule"), not a neutral fact. → The hook frames the fact as rejection: "Nobody wanted him."
- **yt-retention** (no CSV yet, the account has no videos): designed against its three leaks. Hook leak → action and text from frame 1, no setup. Cliffs → every topic change signposted by a game UI event (window, level up, swirl) at most every 3 s. Slide → 20 s, no explanation the picture doesn't already show.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Overworld tour**: a map from Serbia to Denver. Rejected: the travelling eats the 20 s.
2. **Turn-based duel** vs "the draft". Fun, but the battle needs explaining.
3. **Level up on the court** (chosen): the player on a court, each fact a game event: the draft as an "ITEM: PICK 41" window, MVPs as level-ups with a fanfare, the title as an encounter swirl into a gold palette flood. A viewer who has never played a video game still reads it.

## Logline and arc
A big, slow player nobody picked → he plays → he levels up three times → gold. Ends on a YES/NO menu that turns into a comment.

## Benchmark
Learn: the grammar of mid-90s console RPG UI (typing boxes, level-up windows, menu cursor), pixel sports games (side view of the court). Don't take: any game's UI frames, font, melodies, sounds, or any team/league logo. Generic jersey, no logo; number 15 only.

## Cue map
Chiptune at 140 bpm: pulse lead (25 % duty), triangle bass, LFSR noise drums, light echo. Key change on the gold flood. Silence for 1 beat before the swirl.

## Subtitle design
The dialog box IS the subtitle (bottom quarter, above Instagram's UI: box y 200–262 of 320), pixel font, typing at ~38 chars/s with blips; portrait on the left.

## Delivered (changes from the plan)
- 26 s, not 20: the voice lines run longer; every scene still changes within 3–6 s.
- Player drawn at 2× (pixels of 12 real px) so he reads as the protagonist on a phone; UI and court stay at 1×.
- Music −8 dB under the narrator (chip voices are dense).
