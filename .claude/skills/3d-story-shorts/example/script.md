## Voice-over

**Chapter 1 - The Hidden Flaw (0:00)**

In 1978, welders secretly patched a brand new New York skyscraper, every single night. It stood fifty-nine stories high on giant stilts, and it had just opened. The building rules only asked about wind hitting the flat sides. But a student asked about corner winds, and the numbers showed about forty percent more force. Then the engineer learned the builders had swapped his welded joints for bolts to save money. A big enough storm could snap those bolts, right in the middle of Manhattan.

**Chapter 2 - The Secret Repair (0:25)**

You see, a weld turns a joint into one solid piece. Bolts are only as strong as the bolts. Every night after the offices emptied, welders covered the bolted joints with steel plates. During the repair, Hurricane Ella headed for New York, and evacuation plans were drawn up. So everyone watched the forecasts, until the storm turned away. The repair worked, and the public didn't hear about it until 1995.

## Left out on purpose

- The student's name: accounts differ on who called (Diane Hartley's thesis vs. an architecture student LeMessurier described).
- The exact wind speed that could topple it and "once every X years" odds: sources give different numbers.
- Repair cost: sources range from "several million" to $12 million.
- No invented quotes from real people.

## Wording to watch

- "Patched" (not "welded back together"): the building was reinforced, not broken.
- "Builders" = the contractors who made the swap; the engineer's office had approved it, so don't call anyone a villain.
- LeMessurier is pronounced "le-MEZH-ur-ay" if you add his name back.
